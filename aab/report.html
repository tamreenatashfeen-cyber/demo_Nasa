REPORT

Candidate flood change

Radar suggests a small area of possible flooding in Kandia Gol, about 0.0278 km², roughly four football fields. Most of the change it detected looks like disturbed ground, not standing water. This is a candidate estimate, not confirmed floodwater, and it is meant to guide field checks, not replace them.

EVIDENCE

What the data shows

What the radar pair supports, and what it cannot prove.

What the data shows

  • 55 small patches, 0.0278 km² in total, on a 10 m grid.
  • The two scenes form a valid same-track pair (Track 84, Frame 19, ascending).
  • A much larger area, 11.56 km², got brighter. That points to surface change, not standing water.
  • The event itself is confirmed only qualitatively, by the PDMA KP situation report.

What it cannot prove

  • That any patch is confirmed floodwater.
  • How large the flood was at its peak. No satellite imaged the area between 19 and 24 July.
  • That the brighter areas are debris. Radar cannot tell debris from other surface changes.
  • That the real extent is not larger. Debris-laden flash floods may be under-counted.
  • Anything against ground truth. No optical or field data was used.

LIMITATIONS

Limitations

Select a heading for detail.

01The peak was never imaged.

No satellite passed over between 19 July (14:03 UTC) and 24 July (13:54 UTC). The 26 July scene shows conditions about four days after the cloudburst.

02Flash floods scour more than they pool.

Fast mountain floods move boulders and debris, which make the surface rougher and brighter on radar, instead of leaving calm, dark water. PDMA reported 7 to 8 houses washed away.

03Steep valleys hide parts of the scene.

Radar shadow and layover cover about 14% of the 5 km study area and can hide narrow stretches of river.

04Narrow streams are too small for the grid.

Torrents narrower than about 20 m blend with their banks at 10 m resolution, and the cleaning step can remove them.

05Provisional data, no ground truth.

Both NISAR products are provisional, and the classification uses physical thresholds without an independent flood outline.
